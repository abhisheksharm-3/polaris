# Polaris 1.6.0 — release notes

## Written for

Individual senior/staff engineers and small-team tech leads (1–8 people) who already run Claude Code
as their primary dev surface and have hand-written a `CLAUDE.md`. You install plugins from
marketplaces and already carry companions like `superpowers`, `frontend-design`, and stack skills
(evidence: `.claude-plugin/plugin.json` dependencies; Claude Code discover-plugins docs). Before this,
you were pasting the same rules into every session, then hand-reviewing agent output for
over-building and machine-sounding prose, or stitching Spec Kit or BMAD-METHOD onto Claude Code for
structure. You want one enforced standard instead of a prompt you retype (evidence: `README.md`,
`rules/core.md`). Team size is inferred from the single-author, per-project `.polaris/config.json`
design, not from user data — correct this line if it is wrong about you.

## The headline

Ask for release notes and get two documents instead of a diff to summarize yourself: one for your dev
team and client, one for the people who use your product — `/polaris:notes`.

## What's new

- **Release notes without staring at a commit log.** Run `/polaris:notes` before you ship, and it
  works out which commits belong to the release, shows you the range, and asks you to confirm before
  it reads further. If your team ships by tag, merged-PR window, or Jira fix version instead, tell it
  at that prompt and it uses that.
- **Notes grounded in what changed, not what the commit message claims.** It opens the diff behind
  any commit whose message doesn't already say what happened — no "fix" or "wip" entries copied
  straight into your notes.
- **Two documents from one pass, aimed at two different readers.** One document for your dev team and
  client covers what changed and what to do about it. The other is written for the people who use your
  product, in their words, and shows the audience it wrote for at the top so you can correct it before
  you send it.
- **Nothing goes out the door on its own.** It never posts to GitHub, Notion, or Slack until you say
  so, and only to the destination you name.
- **A release with nothing to tell your users gets refused, not padded.** If everything in the range
  is internal-only, it stops rather than inventing a feature that didn't ship.

## Heads up

Polaris runs from an installed plugin cache. `/polaris:notes` becomes callable after your next plugin
update — landing in the repo is not enough on its own.
